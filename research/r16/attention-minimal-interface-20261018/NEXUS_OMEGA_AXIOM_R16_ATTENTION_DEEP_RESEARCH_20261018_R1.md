## Independent literature and correction
- Leroy (2009), DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2009.04.002: task-switching attention residue demonstrated in two studies, not universal.
- Leroy & Glomb (2018), DOI 10.1287/orsc.2017.1184: ready-to-resume plan mitigated context-specific interruption cost in four studies. Motivates testable opt-in operator checkpoint.
- Mark, Gudith & Klocke (2008), DOI 10.1145/1357054.1357072: some interrupted tasks completed faster but with greater stress/effort; time only is insufficient.
- Nguyen et al. (2025), DOI 10.1037/bul0000498, PubMed 41231585: 71 studies, 98,299 participants; SFV associated with poorer cognition r≈−.34, attention −.38, inhibition −.41, mental health −.21. CORRELATION ONLY; no direct NEXUS effectiveness claim.
- Mark 47-second screen dwelling is descriptive interview-based context, not a universal attention span or preregistered effect estimate.
- NASA TLX: six subjective workload dimensions; W3C WCAG 2.2 2.2.2 and 2.3.3; PROV-O provenance: appropriate independent design controls, not efficacy evidence.

## Critical scientific separation
R15 is data plane: tile identifiers, transferred response body bytes, decoder memory, GPU/CPU and energy. R16 is operator outcomes: correct source rank, HOLD detection, task switch recovery, errors, workload. **Reducing tiles cannot be equated to reducing cognitive load**. Counterexamples include fewer tiles with frequent distracting toasts, a fast static map hiding HOLD, or a high-byte static prefetch without interruptions.
